Background & Notation

Let $p = 2 - i$ and $q = 2 + i$ in the Gaussian integers $\mathbb{Z}[i]$. Note that $p\bar{p} = 5$, and $p, q$ are non-associate prime elements with $\gcd(p, q) = 1$. Let $V_1 = \mathbb{Z}[i]_{(p)}$ and $V_2 = \mathbb{Z}[i]_{(q)}$ be the corresponding discrete valuation rings inside $K = \mathbb{Q}[i]$. Let $V = V_1 \cap V_2$. Then $V$ is a Dedekind domain with exactly two maximal ideals, $pV$ and $qV$, and is in fact a principal ideal domain (PID).

Let $M = \mathbb{Q}[i]/V_1$, viewed as a $V$-module. Puninski studies the trivial extension ring (or upper-triangular formal matrix ring): $$ S = \left\{\begin{pmatrix} \bar{\alpha} & v \\ 0 & \alpha \end{pmatrix} : \alpha \in V,\, v \in M\right\} $$ with componentwise addition and multiplication given by: $$ \begin{pmatrix} \bar{\alpha} & v \\ 0 & \alpha \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} \bar{\beta} & w \\ 0 & \beta \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \overline{\alpha\beta} & \bar{\alpha}w + \beta v \\ 0 & \alpha\beta \end{pmatrix}. $$ For brevity, we represent elements of $S$ as pairs $(\alpha, v)$ with $\alpha \in V$ and $v \in M$, where: $$ (\alpha, v)(\beta, w) = (\alpha\beta,\, \bar{\alpha}w + \beta v). $$ $S$ possesses an involutive anti-automorphism given by $(\alpha, v) \mapsto (\bar{\alpha}, v)$. Consequently, $S$ is right coherent if and only if it is left coherent.

All of $V_1, V_2$ and $V$ are PIDs, being different localizations of the PID $\mathbb Z[i]$

Lemmas

Lemma 1 (Finite Quotients of $V_1$). For any nonzero $x \in V_1$, the quotient $V_1 / xV_1$ is finite, with order $5^k$ for some integer $k \ge 0$.

Proof. $V_1$ is a discrete valuation ring with unique maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m} = p V_1$. Since $5 = (2-i)(2+i) = pq \in \mathfrak{m}$ and $q \notin \mathfrak{m}$ (so $q \in V_1^\times$), the residue field is: $$ V_1 / p V_1 \cong \mathbb{Z}[i] / (2-i)\mathbb{Z}[i] \cong \mathbb{F}_5. $$ Every nonzero ideal of $V_1$ is of the form $x V_1 = \mathfrak{m}^k = p^k V_1$ for some $k \ge 0$. A standard composition series argument (using factors $\mathfrak{m}^j / \mathfrak{m}^{j+1} \cong V_1/\mathfrak{m} \cong \mathbb{F}_5$) shows that $|V_1 / xV_1| = 5^k < \infty$. $\blacksquare$

Lemma 2 (Isomorphism $\frac{1}{x}V_1/V_1 \cong V_1/xV_1$). For any nonzero $x \in V_1$, there is an isomorphism of $V_1$-modules (and hence of $V$-modules): $$ \frac{1}{x}V_1 / V_1 \cong V_1 / xV_1. $$ Consequently, $\frac{1}{x}V_1 / V_1$ is a finite set (having $5^k$ elements), and then clearly f.g. as a $V$-module.

Proof. Define the map $\phi: \frac{1}{x}V_1 \to V_1 / xV_1$ by $\phi(x^{-1}v) = v + xV_1$ for $v \in V_1$. The map is well-defined: if $x^{-1}v = x^{-1}w$, then $v = w$. It is clearly $V_1$-linear and surjective, as any coset $v + xV_1$ is the image of $x^{-1}v \in \frac{1}{x}V_1$. Its kernel consists of all $x^{-1}v$ such that $v \in xV_1$, which is precisely $V_1$. By the first isomorphism theorem, $\frac{1}{x}V_1 / V_1 \cong V_1 / xV_1$. By Lemma 1, this module is finite. Being a finite abelian group, it is also finitely generated (and in fact generated by $x^{-1} + V_1$) over $V$. $\blacksquare$

Lemma 3 (Coherence in Bézout Rings). Let $R$ be a right Bézout ring (i.e., every finitely generated right ideal of $R$ is principal). Then $R$ is right coherent if and only if the right annihilator $\operatorname{ann}_r(x)$ is finitely generated for every element $x \in R$.

Proof. Recall that a ring $R$ is right coherent if and only if every finitely generated right ideal is finitely presented (Lam, Lectures on Modules and Rings, Theorem 4.46). For any $x \in R$, the principal right ideal $xR$ is isomorphic as an $R$-module to $R / \operatorname{ann}_r(x)$. Because $R$ is a cyclic free module, $R / \operatorname{ann}_r(x)$ is finitely presented if and only if $\operatorname{ann}_r(x)$ is finitely generated. Since $R$ is right Bézout, every finitely generated right ideal of $R$ is principal, so every finitely generated right ideal is finitely presented if and only if every principal right ideal is finitely presented, which holds if and only if $\operatorname{ann}_r(x)$ is finitely generated for all $x \in R$. $\blacksquare$

Lemma 4 (Ideals of the Form $I \times M$ are Principal). Let $I$ be any nonzero ideal of $V$. We claim that the right ideal $I \times M$ of $S$ is principal, namely that when $I = cV$ for some nonzero $c \in V$, then: $$ I \times M = (c, 0)S. $$

Proof. Since $V$ is a PID, $I = cV$ for some nonzero $c \in V$. For any $(\beta, w) \in S$, we have: $$ (c, 0)(\beta, w) = (c\beta, \bar{c}w). $$ Since $\beta$ ranges over $V$, $c\beta$ ranges over $cV = I$. Next, consider the action of $\bar{c}$ on $M = \mathbb{Q}[i]/V_1$. Since $c \ne 0$, $\bar{c} \ne 0$. The module $\mathbb{Q}[i]$ is divisible as a $\mathbb{Z}[i]$-module, so the quotient module $M = \mathbb{Q}[i]/V_1$ is also divisible. In particular, multiplication by $\bar{c}$ is a surjective endomorphism of $M$: for any $m \in M$, there exists $w \in M$ such that $\bar{c}w = m$. It follows that as $(\beta, w)$ ranges over $S$, $(c, 0)(\beta, w) = (c\beta, \bar{c}w)$ covers all pairs in $I \times M$. Therefore, $I \times M = (c, 0)S$, showing that $I \times M$ is a principal right ideal. $\blacksquare$

Main Result

Theorem. The ring $S$ is both left and right coherent.

Proof. By Puninski's Lemma 7.2(3), $S$ is a Bézout ring. By Lemma 3, to establish that $S$ is right coherent, it suffices to prove that the right annihilator $\operatorname{ann}_r(x)$ is finitely generated for every $x \in S$.

Let $x = (\alpha, v) \in S$. An element $y = (\beta, w) \in S$ belongs to $\operatorname{ann}_r(x)$ if and only if $xy = 0$, which is equivalent to the system: $$ \begin{align} \alpha\beta &= 0 \quad \text{in } V, \\ \bar{\alpha}w + \beta v &= 0 \quad \text{in } M. \end{align} $$ Since $V$ is an integral domain, we distinguish two cases:

Case 1: $\alpha \ne 0$.
Since $V$ is a domain and $\alpha \ne 0$, the equation $\alpha\beta = 0$ implies $\beta = 0$. Substituting $\beta = 0$ into the second equation yields $\bar{\alpha}w = 0$ in $M = \mathbb{Q}[i]/V_1$. Writing $w = \tilde{w} + V_1$ with $\tilde{w} \in \mathbb{Q}[i]$, the condition $\bar{\alpha}w = 0$ means $\bar{\alpha}\tilde{w} \in V_1$, or equivalently $\tilde{w} \in \frac{1}{\bar{\alpha}}V_1$. Thus: $$ \operatorname{ann}_r(x) = \left\{(0, w) : w \in \frac{1}{\bar{\alpha}}V_1 / V_1\right\}. $$ By Lemma 2, the submodule $\frac{1}{\bar{\alpha}}V_1 / V_1$ is a finite set (having order a power of 5). Thus, $\operatorname{ann}_r(x)$ is finite, and is therefore finitely generated (even as an abelian group, and hence as an $S$-module).

Case 2: $\alpha = 0$.
Here $x = (0, v)$. The condition $\alpha\beta = 0$ is trivially satisfied for all $\beta \in V$, and the second equation reduces to: $$ \beta v = 0 \quad \text{in } M. $$ If $v = 0$, then $x = 0$ and $\operatorname{ann}_r(x) = S = (1, 0)S$, which is principal. If $v \ne 0$, then the elements $\beta \in V$ satisfying $\beta v = 0$ form the annihilator ideal: $$ I = \operatorname{ann}_V(v) = \{\beta \in V : \beta v = 0 \text{ in } M\}. $$ Since $v = \tilde{v} + V_1$ with $\tilde{v} \notin V_1$, write $\tilde{v} = u / p^k$ with $k \ge 1$ and $u \in V_1^\times$. Then $\beta \tilde{v} \in V_1 \iff p^k \mid \beta$ in $V_1$, so $I = p^k V_1 \cap V$, which is a nonzero ideal of the PID $V$. Thus $I = cV$ for some nonzero $c \in V$. Since $w \in M$ is completely unconstrained, the annihilator is: $$ \operatorname{ann}_r((0, v)) = I \times M = cV \times M. $$ By Lemma 4, this right ideal is principal: $\operatorname{ann}_r((0, v)) = (c, 0)S$, so it is finitely generated.

Having shown that $\operatorname{ann}_r(x)$ is finitely generated for every $x \in S$, Lemma 3 implies that $S$ is right coherent. Finally, by the involutive anti-automorphism $(\alpha, v) \mapsto (\bar{\alpha}, v)$, $S$ is also left coherent. $\blacksquare$

Note on the Flaw in Puninski's Lemma 7.2(5)

In the proof of Lemma 7.2(5) (p. 244), Puninski computes the right annihilator of $r = (2+i, 0)$ as $(0, \pi(\frac{1}{2-i})V_1)$ and asserts that this ideal is not finitely generated because $\frac{1}{2-i}V_1/V_1$ is not finitely generated as a $V$-module. As shown by Lemma 2, $\frac{1}{2-i}V_1/V_1 \cong V_1/(2-i)V_1 \cong \mathbb{F}_5$, which is a finite module of order 5, and hence trivially finitely generated.
