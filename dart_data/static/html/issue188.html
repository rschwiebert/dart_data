Proof

In Cozzens' paper on page 69 he states:

Also note that $x_0$ and $x_0^2 \notin E$. (See [1].)

This statement is wrong, and it leads to his resulting construction not being simple. As far as I can tell, this has never been pointed out before. (Cohn's later correct construction cites Cozzens without mentioning it.) At the bottom of pg 66/top of pg 67, he states:

If $\delta$ is an inner $\rho$-derivation of $k$ determined by $x \in k$, then one easily checks that the principal left ideal of $R = k[t; \rho, \delta], R(t+x)$ is two-sided and non-trivial.

I show below that $x_0 \in E$. Since $\delta(a) = \rho(a)x_0 - x_0a$ by definition (pg 69), $\delta$ is an inner $\rho$-derivation of $E$, so by the remark above (which holds for $k$ non-commutative by (*)) his constructed ring is not simple.

We start with some simple computations:

$$ \begin{align} x_1x_1 = t_{11} Y t_{11} Y = t_{11} \sigma(t_{11}) Y^2 &= t_{11} t_{12} Y^2 \\ x_1x_2 = t_{11} Y t_{21} Y = t_{11} \sigma(t_{21}) Y^2 &= t_{11} t_{22} Y^2 \\ [x_1, x_0] = x_2 x_0 - x_0 x_1 = (t_{21} \sigma(t_{01}) Y^2 - t_{01} \sigma(t_{11}) Y^2) &= (t_{21} t_{02} - t_{01} t_{12}) Y^2 \\ [x_2, x_0] = x_3 x_0 - x_0 x_2 &= (t_{31} t_{02} - t_{01} t_{22}) Y^2 \end{align} $$

Now we define:

$$ \begin{align} a &:= x_2 x_1^{-1} = t_{21} Y (Y^{-1} t_{11}^{-1}) &= t_{21} t_{11}^{-1} \\ b &:= x_3 x_1^{-1} &= t_{31} t_{11}^{-1} \\ c &:= x_1 x_2 x_1^{-2} = t_{11} t_{22} Y^2 (Y^{-2} t_{12}^{-1} t_{11}^{-1}) &= t_{22} t_{12}^{-1} \\ g_1 &:= [x_1, x_0] x_1^{-2} &= (t_{21} t_{02} - t_{01} t_{12}) / (t_{11} t_{12}) \\ g_2 &:= [x_2, x_0] x_1^{-2} &= (t_{31} t_{02} - t_{01} t_{22}) / (t_{11} t_{12}) \end{align} $$

By definition $x_1, x_2, x_3, [x_1, x_0], [x_2, x_0] \in E$, so then $a, b, c, g_1, g_2 \in E$ as well. Now we can compute that $(ag_2 - bg_1)(b - ac)^{-1} x_1 = x_0$. Here $b - ac$ is invertible because it is a rational function with non-zero numerator since the terms are independent indeterminates. Using sympy, we find that $(ag_2 - bg_1)(b - ac)^{-1} = t_{01}t_{11}^{-1}$. So the left-hand side can be computed as:

$$t_{01}t_{11}^{-1} x_1 = t_{01}t_{11}^{-1} t_{11} Y = t_{01} Y = x_0$$

as desired.

(*) $\delta(a) = \rho(a)x_0 - x_0 a$ forces $(t + x_0)a = ta + x_0a = \rho(a)t + \delta(a) + x_0a = \rho(a)t + \rho(a)x_0 - x_0a + x_0a = \rho(a)(t + x_0)$, so we can derive that $R(t+x_0)$ is a proper two-sided ideal. $\square$
